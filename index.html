Pick a real problem that needs R&D-based deep tech, and submit it online by 25 November 2026.

Step 2

Expert review

Expert Committee reviews the following

  • Clear problem with quantified baseline and targets
  • Genuine deep-tech need
  • Scale of public or strategic national interest and impact
  • Data, test-bed and experts you can offer
  • A credible deployment path
Step 3

Published as a challenge

Shortlisted statements are finalised by MSH and, once approved by MeitY, published as challenges.

Step 4

Startups apply

Deep-tech startups apply through MeitY-approved incubators, teamed with academia or an R&D lab.

  • DPIIT-recognised and at least 51% Indian-owned
Step 5

Built and deployed

Selected projects run for 2 to 5 years through four milestones, with MeitY funding up to 50% of the project cost.

  • M1 Research and M2 Product development
  • M3 Go-to-market and M4 Scaling
  • Piloted in your setting
  • Product/solution deployed if it meets the agreed metrics

Submission does not guarantee selection.

Frequently asked questions

What is ADAMYA?

ADAMYA (Acceleration of Deep Tech Startups by MeitY with Academia and Industry) is an enabling platform for deep-tech startups to undertake research-based technology and product development, using the expertise and infrastructure of academia, R&D institutions and industry. It works through selected MeitY-approved deep-tech incubators, and is executed by MeitY Startup Hub.

What is a problem statement?

A clear description of a real problem your organisation or sector faces: how it is handled today, its scale and cost, why it needs deep tech, what the solution should achieve, and how you would support and deploy it. It does not describe a solution or product.

Who can submit a problem statement?

Central Ministries/Departments, CPSUs/PSUs/UT Departments, autonomous or statutory bodies and other government undertakings, as well as startups, industry (SME/Large), VCs/Angel networks/Investors, industry associations and others. All submit through the same form.

What does the form ask for?

Your organisation and nodal person; the problem, its current situation, scale and cost; why it needs deep tech and which technology areas; what the solution should achieve and any constraints; the support you offer the solver startup; and your deployment timeline. It ends with the no-conflict declaration and the signed Declaration upload.

How are submitted problem statements evaluated?

The Expert Committee reviews all statements. Indicative criteria:

  • clarity of the problem, with quantified baseline and targets
  • a genuine deep-tech need
  • scale of public or strategic national interest and impact
  • data, test-bed and expert support offered
  • credibility of the deployment
  • fit with priority sectors
What happens after a problem statement is shortlisted?

Shortlisted statements are finalised, once approved are published as challenges. Eligible startups then submit solution proposals.

Is a signed declaration required?

Yes. Upload the Declaration, signed by the nodal person for the problem statement. Unsigned submissions are not screened.

What is the submission deadline?

Submissions are accepted online from 31 October 2026 to 25 November 2026.

Can an organisation submit multiple problem statements?

Yes. An organisation can submit more than one problem statement by filling in a separate online form for each one, with its own signed Declaration. All forms must be submitted by 25 November 2026.
